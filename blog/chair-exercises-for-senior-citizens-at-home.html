Chair Exercises for Senior Citizens at Home: A Simple Indian Guide

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
7 min readUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The short answer

  • Chair exercises for senior citizens are a safe way to start: no gym, little space and easy to adjust.
  • You need a sturdy chair without wheels, closed shoes and about 10 to 15 minutes.
  • Move slowly, stay pain-free and check with your doctor first if you have any health condition.

After 60, the goal is not to look like an athlete. It is to stand up from a chair easily, climb stairs with confidence and enjoy everyday life with your family. Chair exercises are one of the safest ways to begin. This guide gives you simple seated movements, a 10-minute morning routine and tips that suit Indian homes.

Why chair exercises suit seniors

  • You can start where you are, even if standing or walking is difficult.
  • The chair gives support, so you feel steadier and more confident.
  • You need no equipment and very little space.
  • Gentle movement helps keep joints moving and builds the habit of daily activity.

Before you start

Please talk to your doctor or physiotherapist first if any of these apply to you:

  • A recent fall, or dizziness or fainting when you stand or exercise.
  • Chest discomfort, unusual breathlessness or new weakness.
  • Recent surgery, a fracture or a serious illness.
  • Osteoporosis, or heart, lung, kidney or neurological conditions.

Set up a safe space

  • Use a sturdy chair without wheels. Place it against a wall so it cannot slide.
  • Clear rugs, cords and anything you could trip on.
  • Wear closed, non-slip footwear. Avoid bare feet or socks on smooth floors.
  • Keep a glass of water and your phone within reach.

Seated cat-cow

This gentle back movement helps you feel less stiff in the morning. Sit on the chair with your feet flat. Lengthen your spine and open your chest, then slowly round your upper back a little. Return to neutral between each movement. Do 5 to 8 slow cycles in a small range. If you have osteoporosis or a spinal fracture, avoid rounding forward and ask your doctor what is safe.

Shoulder rolls

Relax your arms, lift your shoulders gently, roll them backward and lower them. Do 6 to 10 repetitions. If your shoulders are stiff, make the circles smaller. Never move into pain.

Seated marching

Sit tall with your feet flat. Lift one foot a little, lower it and swap legs, like marching on the spot. Do 8 to 12 lifts per leg. If your knee or hip is limited, lift only the heel. This keeps your legs active, which helps when you stand and walk.

A 10 to 15 minute morning routine

Do these in order, slowly and with relaxed breathing. Rest between exercises whenever you wish.

  1. 2 minutesSeated tall posture and comfortable breathing.
  2. 2 minutesShoulder rolls and seated cat-cow.
  3. 2 minutesSeated side reach, both sides.
  4. 3 minutesSeated marching.
  5. 2 minutesAnkle circles, then gentle trunk rotation.
Sarita's tip 🌿

Link it to something you already do every day, like right after your morning pooja or before your first cup of chai. A habit that fits your day is easier to keep.

How often should you do them?

Start with 2 or 3 sessions a week and add more as you feel comfortable. The WHO suggests older adults work toward 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening on at least two days, when their health allows. These are long-term goals, not where you must begin. Read the WHO physical activity fact sheet for details.

Make it work in an Indian home

  • Sitting on the floor→A sturdy dining chair with a flat seat
  • A light plastic chair that slides→The same chair placed against a wall
  • Bare feet on marble or tiles→Closed, non-slip footwear
  • Exercising in the heat→A cooler hour, with water nearby

For sons and daughters: how to help

  • Sit with your parent for the first few sessions and exercise together.
  • Check the chair and clear the floor before each session.
  • Praise effort and consistency, not how many repetitions they do.
  • Ask their doctor before starting if there are health concerns.

When to stop and get help

  • Chest pain, fainting or severe dizziness.
  • Unusual shortness of breath or sudden new weakness.
  • Sharp pain that gets worse.

Stop the exercise and seek medical help straight away if any of these happen.

Frequently asked questions

Are chair exercises enough to stay fit after 60?

They are a safe starting point and can build mobility, strength and confidence. Over time, many people add supported standing exercises, walking and strength work as their health allows. Your doctor or physiotherapist can advise what suits you.

How often should seniors do chair exercises?

Many people start with 2 or 3 short sessions a week and build up slowly. The morning routine in this guide takes 10 to 15 minutes. Rest whenever you need to and never push through pain.

Can I do chair exercises with arthritis or knee pain?

Gentle, pain-free movement is often suitable, but advice depends on the person. Use a small range, skip any movement that hurts, and check with your doctor or physiotherapist first.

What kind of chair is best?

A sturdy chair without wheels, with a flat seat at a height where your feet rest flat on the floor. Armrests can help. Place it against a wall or on a non-slip surface so it cannot slide.

Can chair exercises help with balance and falls?

Seated movements build strength and body awareness and are a safe place to begin. Balance practice also needs supported standing work, with a fixed counter and someone nearby. If you are worried about falls, ask your doctor or physiotherapist.

Can my parent do these exercises alone?

Many can, once a doctor has said it is fine and the chair is safe. For the first few sessions, it helps if a family member is nearby.
