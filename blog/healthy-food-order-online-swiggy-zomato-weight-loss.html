Healthy Food Order Online: What to Pick on Swiggy and Zomato Without Wrecking Weight Loss

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
9 min readUpdated 22 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • You can lose weight and still order online. Build each order as protein first, vegetables next, one measured carb.
  • Pick tandoori, grilled, dal, idli and curd. Skip cream gravies, fried starters and sweet drinks.
  • Use the apps' health tools, but always check the portion. Get the free "Order This, Not That" cheat sheet below.

Back-to-back meetings, a late commute, a fridge with nothing in it: for many working women, ordering food online is not a choice, it is survival. The good news is that you do not have to give it up to lose weight. The problem is rarely the app. It is the default choices: a large portion, a creamy gravy, a fried side and a sweet drink. This guide shows what to pick on Swiggy, Zomato and similar apps, and what to skip.

The apps now help you choose

  • Zomato launched a "Healthy Mode" that uses AI and restaurant data to score dishes on nutrition and lets you filter menus.
  • Swiggy has an "EatRight" collection with over 1.8 million dishes from more than 200,000 restaurants, and has added ingredient and cooking-method details.
  • FSSAI has asked food platforms to show calorie and allergen information for larger restaurant chains, those with ten or more outlets.

These tools change often and differ by city, so check your own app. Treat a score as a guide, not a guarantee. A dish can score well and still come in a large portion or with a rich sauce.

Why a "healthy-sounding" order can wreck your week

  • Hidden fat. Ghee, butter and cream make restaurant curries far richer than home food. A creamy takeaway curry can have several times the calories of a home-style one.
  • Big portions. A large restaurant naan can be twice the size of a home roti, and a restaurant biryani plate is often quoted at roughly 500 to 900 kcal.
  • Fried and sweet extras. A starter, a cola and a dessert can add as much as the main meal.
  • Numbers vary hugely. The same dish can differ several times between restaurants, so use any calorie figure as a rough guide only.

The 4-step ordering formula

  1. Protein firstGrilled or tandoori chicken, fish or paneer, dal, egg, chana, sprouts or curd.
  2. Add vegetablesA salad, sabzi or soup, so half your meal is plants.
  3. One measured carb1 or 2 rotis, a small portion of rice or 2 or 3 idli, not all three.
  4. Skip or share the extrasFried starters, sweet drinks and dessert. Pick one, not all.

Customise your order

  • Write in the cooking instructions: "less oil, no extra butter, sauces and dressing on the side". Not every restaurant will follow it, but many do.
  • Ask for extra salad or cucumber and a bowl of curd or raita.
  • Choose plain roti instead of naan, and skip the butter on top.
  • Pick a "half" or "regular" portion when it is offered.
  • Check the allergen information if you have an allergy.

The portion rules for delivery food

  • Half-box rule: eat half the rice or roti, and save the rest for tomorrow. Refrigerate leftovers promptly and reheat them only once.
  • Eat the protein and salad first.
  • Plate it. Do not eat straight from the box at your desk.
  • Drink water first, and decide your portion before you open the box.

Office lunch and late-night survival

  • Order before you are starving. Plan by 11 am, so you are not choosing the heaviest dish at 2.30 pm.
  • Look for home-style options: a simple dal, sabzi, roti and curd meal often beats a restaurant combo.
  • Keep a desk snack, such as roasted chana, a fruit or curd. See our healthy Indian evening snacks.
  • Late at night, choose light: khichdi with curd, dal with one or two rotis, idli with sambar or a clear soup with a grilled protein.

How often is okay?

Aim for balance, not perfection. Plan two or three delivery meals a week, and make them good ones. An occasional biryani is fine. What matters is your overall week, not one meal. Sarita's tip 🌿

Decide before you open the app: "protein, vegetables, one carb". Having your formula ready stops the scroll-and-crave that leads to the heaviest dish on the menu.

Keep it safe and realistic

  • If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, a food allergy or a thyroid or PCOS condition, ask your doctor or dietitian what suits you.
  • If you have had an eating disorder, do not use calorie counting without professional support.
  • Avoid crash diets and "detox" meals. They do not last.

Frequently asked questions

Which app is healthier, Swiggy or Zomato?

Neither app is healthy or unhealthy by itself. What matters is the dish, the portion and the extras you choose. Both now offer health tools, such as Zomato's Healthy Mode and Swiggy's EatRight collection, but features vary by city and change often.

Can I lose weight while ordering food online?

Yes. What counts is your overall week. Choose protein and vegetables first, one measured portion of carbs, skip fried extras and sweet drinks, and keep delivery meals to a planned number each week.

How many calories are in a restaurant meal?

It varies enormously. The same dish can differ several times between restaurants because of ghee, butter, cream and portion size. For example, a plate of biryani is often quoted at roughly 500 to 900 kcal. Use the calorie information in the app when it is shown, and treat any other number as a rough guide.

Is biryani okay on a weight-loss plan?

Occasionally, yes. Order a half or regular portion, add raita and a big salad, skip the fried starter and the sweet dessert, and eat the protein and salad first.

What is a good light dinner to order late at night?

Moong dal khichdi with curd, dal with one or two rotis, idli with sambar, a clear soup with a grilled protein and salad, or a tandoori platter without the naan. Avoid ordering when you are starving, because hunger pushes you toward the heaviest option.

Are meals labelled healthy always healthy?

Not always. Check the ingredients and portion, and watch for creamy dressings, extra cheese, fried toppings and sweet sauces. A health score or label is a helpful guide, not a guarantee.
