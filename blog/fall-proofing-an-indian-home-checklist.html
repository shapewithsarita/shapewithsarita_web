Fall-Proofing an Indian Home: Bathroom, Stairs, Rugs and Night Path

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The short answer

  • Walk through each room with this fall-proofing checklist and fix the biggest hazards first.
  • Priorities: loose rugs, wet bathroom floors, stairs and handrails, and a lit path to the toilet at night.
  • Print the checklist, tick it off, and repeat every few months.

Falls are a major cause of injury in older adults, and people who fall once are 2 to 3 times more likely to fall again. The good news is that many falls come from hazards at home that are easy to see and fix. This guide gives you a printable fall-proofing checklist made for Indian homes, from the bathroom and stairs to rugs and the night path to the toilet.

Why Indian homes need their own checklist

  • Polished marble and tile floors can be very slippery, especially when wet.
  • Bathrooms are often wet, with buckets, stools and Indian-style toilets.
  • High door sills and uneven steps are easy to miss.
  • Many people sit on the floor for pooja or meals, so getting up is a daily challenge.
  • Power cuts and monsoon rains make floors and paths riskier.
  • Sarees, lungis and long kurtas can catch underfoot.

How to use the checklist

  1. WalkGo through every room with your parent, at the time of day they use it.
  2. TickMark each item you can already say yes to.
  3. FixPick the three biggest hazards and fix them today.
  4. RepeatCheck again every few months and after any fall.

The room-by-room checklist

Entrance and floors

  • Loose rugs and mats removed, or fixed with double-sided tape or a non-slip backing.
  • Floors clear of shoes, bags, buckets and clutter.
  • Cords and wires taped or coiled along the wall.
  • Door sills and uneven steps marked with bright tape so they are easy to see.
  • Wet floor after mopping kept off-limits until completely dry.

Bathroom

  • Non-slip mat inside and outside the shower area.
  • Grab bars next to the toilet and in the shower, fixed firmly to the wall.
  • A stable stool or chair for bathing.
  • Floor wiped dry after every bath.
  • Night light on. Toilet access checked if squatting is difficult.

Bedroom and night path

  • Clear, lit path from bed to toilet.
  • Lamp or torch within reach of the bed, plus a phone.
  • Bed height allows feet to rest flat when sitting.
  • Mosquito net and bedsheet kept clear of the walking path.
  • A habit of sitting at the bed edge for a minute before standing.

Stairs and steps

  • Handrails on both sides, firm and as long as the stairs.
  • Light at the top and bottom, with switches at both ends.
  • Nothing kept on the steps.
  • Edges marked, with non-slip strips or treads.
  • Outdoor and building steps well lit at night.

Living area

  • Furniture arranged for easy walking, with no low tables in the path.
  • A sturdy chair with armrests for getting up easily.
  • Fan, charger and TV wires out of the walkway.
  • An emergency light or torch ready for power cuts.

Kitchen and puja room

  • Oil and water spills wiped at once.
  • Daily items stored between hip and shoulder height.
  • No climbing on stools or chairs to reach shelves.
  • A low stool or chair available if sitting on the floor is hard.

Clothing and footwear

  • Closed, non-slip footwear worn indoors.
  • No bare feet or socks on smooth floors.
  • Long hems, saree pleats and lungi ends kept off the floor.
  • Spectacles clean and prescription up to date.

Emergency

  • Family and doctor numbers saved on the phone and posted on the fridge.
  • A neighbour or relative knows where the spare key is.

Low-cost fixes you can do this weekend

  • Loose rugs→Remove them, or tape them down
  • Dark path at night→Plug-in night lights from bed to toilet
  • Slippery bathroom floor→Non-slip mats and a stable bathing stool
  • High shelves→Move daily items between hip and shoulder height
  • Hard-to-see steps→Bright tape or non-slip strips on the edges

For grab bars and handrails, ask a carpenter or plumber to fix them firmly into the wall. Towel rails and suction-cup bars can come loose and are not safe to lean on.

The night path matters most

Many older people get up at night to use the toilet, when they are sleepy, the room is dark and blood pressure may drop as they stand. Keep a lamp or torch by the bed, a lit and clear path to the toilet, and a night light in the bathroom. Teach your parent to sit at the edge of the bed for a minute before standing up.

Sarita's tip 🌿

Do the checklist together, not as an inspection. Ask your parent, "Where do you feel unsteady?" They often know the problem spot already.

Fall-proof the body too

A safe home works best together with stronger legs and better balance. Try our balance exercises for seniors and chair exercises. Ask the doctor to review medicines that cause dizziness, and get eyes checked regularly. Sons and daughters can read how to help parents exercise safely.

After a fall: when to see a doctor

  • Any fall with injury, severe pain or swelling.
  • A blow to the head, confusion or drowsiness.
  • Repeated falls or near falls, even if small.
  • Dizziness, fainting or chest pain before the fall.

If your parent has had more than one fall, ask a doctor or physiotherapist for a proper assessment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common cause of falls at home?

Common causes are loose rugs, clutter, wet or slippery floors, poor lighting, stairs without good handrails, and unsafe footwear. Many of these are easy and inexpensive to fix.

Does my parent really need grab bars?

Grab bars next to the toilet and in the shower give support where falls are common. They must be fixed firmly into the wall, so ask a professional to fit them. Towel rails and suction-cup bars can come loose.

Are Indian-style toilets safe for seniors?

They can be difficult if knees, hips or balance are weak. If squatting is hard, ask a doctor or physiotherapist about safer options, such as a raised or western seat with grab bars.

What footwear is best indoors?

Closed or well-fitting, non-slip footwear with a flat sole. Avoid walking in socks or bare feet on smooth floors, and avoid loose slippers that slide off.

How often should I check the home?

Do the full checklist once now, then repeat every few months, after any fall or near fall, and when the season changes, especially before the monsoon.

What should we do after a fall?

If the fall caused injury, a head strike, new confusion or severe pain, get medical help at once. Tell the doctor about any fall, even a small one, because repeated falls need a proper check.
