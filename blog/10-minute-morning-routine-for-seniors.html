Morning routine

A 10-Minute Morning Routine for Seniors

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
7 min readUpdated 31 Oct 2026

The short answer

  • Start slowly: sit on the bed edge, drink water, stand up gently.
  • Then do 10 minutes: seated marching and shoulder rolls, sit-to-stand, heel raises and slow breathing.
  • No equipment, no gym. Get the free printable mini guide below.

Mornings are when many seniors feel stiff, slow and unsure. A short, gentle routine can loosen the joints, wake up the legs and set a calm tone for the day. This is the 10-minute starter day from my handbook Stronger After 60. It uses only a chair and a counter, and it is built to be easy enough that you will actually do it.

Start the day safely: before the routine

When you get up from lying down, blood pressure can drop for a moment, which can cause dizziness, especially in older adults. NHS guidance suggests getting up in stages:

  1. In bedFlex your ankles up and down and breathe deeply for a few minutes. Do a few gentle marching movements.
  2. Sit up slowlyMove from lying to sitting, not straight to standing.
  3. Pause for a minuteSit on the edge of the bed, feet on the floor.
  4. Drink waterA glass of water helps, unless your doctor has limited your fluids.
  5. Stand slowlyHold a support and wait until any dizziness settles before you walk.

If you feel dizzy, stop, sit down and drink some water. If it happens often, tell your doctor, because your blood pressure and medicines may need a review.

Talk to your doctor first if

  • You have heart, blood pressure or balance problems.
  • You have felt faint or fallen recently.
  • You have diabetes and take medicines that can cause low sugar.
  • You have had recent surgery or a serious illness.

The 10-minute routine

If a full session feels like too much, do this short routine and count it as a win. The exercise numbers match the book.

  1. 2 minutesSeated marching (4) and shoulder rolls (2).
  2. 3 minutesSit-to-stand (9), one set.
  3. 2 minutesSupported heel raises (12).
  4. 2 minutesComfortable breathing (6).

Easier and stronger versions

  • Easier (seated only): seated marching, shoulder rolls, ankle circles and breathing. Swap sit-to-stand and heel raises for seated knee extensions.
  • The full wake-up: do all 8 seated yoga movements in order for 10 to 15 minutes. See our chair exercises guide.
  • Stronger: after a couple of weeks, add a few repetitions, one change at a time. Then follow the 12-week plan.

Make it a habit

  • Link it to something you already do: after brushing your teeth, before your morning chai or after pooja.
  • Keep your shoes by the bed and the chair in the same place.
  • Tick your card each day. Do not worry about missed days.
  • In winter, wear something warm. In summer, do it before the heat builds up.
  • Play a favourite bhajan or old song to keep it enjoyable.
Sarita's tip 🌿

If you can only do one thing on a bad day, do the breathing and five sit-to-stands. Count it as a win. A habit grows from small wins.

For sons and daughters

Call or video call at the same time each morning and do the routine together. Check that the chair is sturdy and shoes are non-slip. See our guide on helping elderly parents exercise safely and check the home with our fall-proofing checklist.

Stop right away if

  • Chest pain, fainting or severe dizziness.
  • Unusual shortness of breath or sudden new weakness.
  • Sharp pain that gets worse.

Stop and seek medical help straight away if any of these happen.

Frequently asked questions

Is morning exercise safe for seniors?

Gentle morning movement is safe for many seniors, if they get up slowly, drink water, wear non-slip shoes and keep the effort easy. Blood pressure can drop when standing up quickly, so ask your doctor first if you have heart, blood pressure or balance problems.

Should I exercise before or after morning tea or breakfast?

A short, gentle routine like this suits most people at either time. If you have diabetes or take medicines, ask your doctor whether to eat first, and never exercise if you feel faint or shaky.

What if I feel dizzy when I stand up?

Stop, sit down and drink some water. Stand slowly next time, pausing at the edge of the bed. If dizziness happens often, tell your doctor, because your blood pressure and medicines may need review.

Can I do the routine seated?

Yes. Do seated marching, shoulder rolls, ankle circles and breathing, and swap sit-to-stand and heel raises for seated knee extensions. Stay seated if standing feels unsafe.

How long before I notice a difference?

Many people feel less stiff and more energetic within a couple of weeks. Strength changes take longer, often 6 to 12 weeks. Keep a simple tick chart and be patient.

Can I do more than 10 minutes?

Yes, once this feels easy. You can add more repetitions, do the full 8-move seated yoga sequence for 10 to 15 minutes, or follow the 12-week plan. Add one thing at a time.
