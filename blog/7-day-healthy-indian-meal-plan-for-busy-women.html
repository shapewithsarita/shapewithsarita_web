A 7-Day Healthy Indian Meal Plan for Busy Women

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 22 Oct 2026

The short answer

  • This 7-day healthy Indian meal plan uses food your family already eats: roti, dal, sabzi, curd, eggs and paneer.
  • Weekday dinners take about 20 minutes. One 60-minute Sunday prep covers the week.
  • It is a flexible base. Adjust portions to your body and goal.

Late meetings, school runs, a long commute and a family waiting for dinner: when you are busy, meals become whatever is quickest. This plan is built for that life. It gives you a full week of healthy Indian meals for busy women, with quick breakfasts, tiffin-friendly lunches and simple dinners, so you stop deciding what to cook every evening.

How this meal plan works

  • Protein at every meal: dal, curd, eggs, paneer, tofu, sprouts or chicken and fish.
  • Half the plate is vegetables: sabzi and salad come first.
  • Cook once, eat twice: dal, rajma, chana and batter are made on Sunday.
  • Same food as the family: you change portions, not the whole menu.

Not sure how to fill your plate? See our Indian diet plan for weight loss for the plate method.

  • Half: sabzi and salad (lauki, bhindi, palak, cabbage, cucumber, tomato)
  • A quarter: protein (dal, paneer, curd, eggs, chicken, fish, sprouts, chana)
  • A quarter: 1 to 2 rotis or a small bowl of rice
  • A little fat: a spoon of ghee or oil in cooking is fine. Just measure it.

Sunday prep: 60 minutes that saves your week

  1. CookPressure-cook rajma, kala chana and a pot of toor or moong dal.
  2. PrepareMake chilla, idli and dosa batter. Boil 6 eggs. Soak chana for the week.
  3. ChopWash and chop onions, tomatoes, carrots, cabbage and greens. Store dry in boxes.
  4. Pack snacksDry-roast chana and makhana. Keep in airtight jars.
Sarita's tip 🌿

Do not try to prep everything on week one. Start with just dal and chopped veggies. Add one more thing each Sunday.

Meal prep food safety

Prepped food is only helpful if it is safe. The NHS advises cooling rice quickly, ideally within an hour, keeping it in the fridge for no more than one day, and reheating it only once until steaming hot. For other cooked dishes like dal and sabzi, eat them within two to three days. If you are unsure, cook rice fresh.

Easy swaps for your food choices

  • Egg breakfast→Tofu bhurji or paneer bhurji
  • Chicken or fish curry→Soya chunk, rajma or chana curry
  • Paneer→Tofu or thick curd
  • Rice for dinner→Roti, daliya or khichdi in a small bowl

Office lunch and ordering tips

Pack lunches that travel well, like roti with sabzi and dal, or rajma with rice. Carry salad and curd in a separate small box. When you order in, choose dal, roti, sabzi, grilled or tandoor items, and ask for less oil. Skip cream gravies, fried starters and sweet drinks most days.

How to adjust the plan for your goal

For weight loss, keep the protein and vegetables, reduce roti or rice, and measure oil and sugar. If you feel tired or hungry, add a small portion rather than cutting more. India's national guidelines (ICMR-NIN, 2024) also advise limiting sugar, excess oil and ultra-processed foods. Try our calorie deficit calculator and protein calculator. Frequently asked questions

Can I follow this plan if I have no time to cook every day?

Yes. The plan is built around one 60-minute Sunday prep and dinners that take about 20 minutes. Breakfasts use batter, boiled eggs or quick-cook foods like poha and oats.

How many calories are in this meal plan?

It does not count calories, because needs differ for each woman. Use your plate and portions as the guide, and estimate your own numbers with the free calorie and protein calculators on this site.

Is this meal plan good for weight loss?

It is a balanced base. For weight loss, keep the protein and vegetables, reduce the portion of roti or rice, and watch added oil and sugar. A safe pace for most adults is about 0.5 to 1 kg a week.

Can vegetarians follow this plan?

Yes. Every day has dal, pulses, curd, paneer, tofu or soya chunks as the protein, with egg and chicken or fish options marked for those who eat them.

How long can I keep cooked food in the fridge?

The NHS advises keeping cooked rice for no more than one day. Most other cooked dishes, like dal and sabzi, are best eaten within two to three days. Reheat food only once and make sure it is steaming hot.

Can I pack these meals for office?

Yes. Lunches like roti with sabzi and dal, rajma with rice, pulao with raita and khichdi travel well in a tiffin. Carry salad and curd in a separate small box.
