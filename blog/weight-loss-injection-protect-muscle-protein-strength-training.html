GLP-1 and muscle

On a Weight-Loss Injection? How to Protect Your Muscle With Protein and Strength Training

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
9 min readUpdated 22 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • Weight-loss injections like semaglutide and tirzepatide (sold as Wegovy, Mounjaro and others) can reduce muscle as well as fat. It is a risk you can manage.
  • Experts advise enough protein, often 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg a day, spread across meals, and strength training 2 to 3 days a week.
  • Your prescribing doctor decides your medicine. Get the free muscle-protection checklist below.
Please note: this article does not tell you to start, stop or change any medicine. These are prescription drugs, and your doctor decides what is right for you. We do not sell, prescribe or advise on medicines.

In 2026, weight-loss injections are suddenly everywhere in India. Friends are using them, doctors are talking about them and cheaper versions have made them easier to reach. The number on the scale drops quickly, and that feels wonderful. But the scale does not tell you what you are losing. If part of that weight is muscle, you may end up weaker, tired and more likely to regain the weight later. This guide explains how to protect your muscle with food and strength training, whatever your doctor has prescribed.

What the research says

  • In trials, these medicines reduce body weight by roughly 5 to 18%, with somewhat smaller effects in real-world use.
  • The weight lost includes fat and lean mass. Lean mass includes muscle, but also water and organs, so the real muscle loss varies. It is a particular concern for older people and for anyone already low in muscle.
  • A 2025 joint advisory from four major bodies (the American College of Lifestyle Medicine, the American Society for Nutrition, the Obesity Medicine Association and The Obesity Society) lists the priorities: managing stomach side effects, preventing nutrient deficiencies, and preserving muscle and bone through strength training and enough protein.
  • Reviews suggest protein intakes above 1.2 g per kg a day, spread evenly across meals, combined with aerobic activity and resistance training, help preserve lean mass.
  • Many people stop these medicines within a year, and weight regain is common. People who also received structured nutrition guidance did better and kept going longer.

Why muscle matters

  • Strength and daily energy: stairs, bags, children, long days at work.
  • Bones: muscle and bone loss often go together.
  • Blood sugar and metabolism: muscle takes up and uses glucose.
  • Keeping the weight off: more muscle supports healthier body composition after the medicine ends.
  • Healthy ageing: especially around perimenopause, when women already lose muscle faster.

Protein: how much, and how to fit it in

Many experts suggest at least 1.2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, often 1.2 to 1.6 g, spread across meals. Ask your doctor first if you have kidney disease or another condition that limits protein.

Eating when your appetite is small

  • Eat 3 small meals and 1 or 2 protein snacks, instead of waiting until you feel hungry.
  • On queasy days, choose soft, easy foods: curd, milk, egg, dal, soup, buttermilk and khichdi.
  • Go easy on very greasy or fried food, which can worsen nausea and stomach symptoms.
  • Eat slowly, stop when comfortably full and do not force large meals.
  • Drink fluids regularly, because dehydration is a real risk.
  • Ask your doctor about blood tests for iron, vitamin B12, vitamin D and calcium, since eating less can leave gaps.

Strength training: the other half

Do strength work on 2 or 3 days a week, and walk on the others. A simple week looks like this:

  • Stay about 2 reps short of exhaustion at first, and rest 1 to 2 minutes between sets.
  • Eat before you train, and drink water. Skip hard sessions if you feel faint or nauseated.

Plan for the long term

Medicine can start weight loss. Habits keep it. Protein, strength training, sleep and regular meals are the habits that work with or without a prescription. Build them now, and talk to your doctor about what happens when you stop.

Sarita's tip 🌿

Pick two things for the first month: protein first at every meal, and strength training on three fixed days. Everything else can wait.

Take extra care if

  • You are older, or already have low muscle or bone strength.
  • You have kidney, liver, gallbladder or pancreas problems, or diabetes.
  • You have a history of an eating disorder.
  • You are pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy.
  • You eat a mostly vegetarian diet with little protein.

Your doctor knows your history, so please follow their advice first.

Frequently asked questions

Do semaglutide and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) cause muscle loss?

These medicines reduce body weight, and the loss includes some lean mass as well as fat. Lean mass is not only muscle, and the share varies from person to person. Experts say it is a risk to manage with enough protein and strength training, not a certainty.

How much protein do I need on a weight-loss injection?

Many experts suggest at least 1.2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, often 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg, spread evenly across meals. People with kidney disease or other conditions may need a different amount, so ask your doctor or dietitian before changing your protein.

Can I do strength training while on these injections?

Yes, for most people, and it is encouraged. Start gently, build up slowly, stay hydrated and eat before you train. Stop and tell your doctor if you feel faint, very weak or unwell.

What if I have almost no appetite?

Eat small, frequent meals and put protein first on the plate. Choose easy options like curd, milk, egg, dal, paneer, soup and buttermilk. Do not force large meals, and tell your doctor if you cannot eat or drink enough.

Will I regain weight if I stop the injection?

Regaining weight after stopping is common, and many people stop within a year. Building habits now, with protein, strength training, good sleep and regular meals, gives you the best chance of keeping what you lose. Talk to your doctor about a plan for stopping.

Do I need protein powder?

Food comes first, and most people can meet their needs with Indian foods. A supplement may help if your appetite is very low, but ask your doctor or dietitian first, especially if you have kidney problems, and check the label for added sugar.
