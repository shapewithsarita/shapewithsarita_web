Vegetarian Seniors: Getting Enough Protein and Vitamin B12

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 19 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • Protein: dal, curd, milk, paneer, soya, tofu and sprouts can meet your needs if every meal has a protein source.
  • Vitamin B12: plant foods have none naturally, and older adults often absorb it poorly. Ask your doctor about a blood test.
  • Do not start high-dose supplements on your own.

Many Indian seniors are vegetarian by faith and habit, and a vegetarian diet can be healthy and nourishing. After 60, though, two nutrients need extra care: protein, to protect muscle, and vitamin B12, which is not found in dal or vegetables. This guide shows vegetarian seniors and their families how to cover both.

Part 1: Protein on a vegetarian plate

Many expert groups suggest at least 1.0 to 1.2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day for healthy older adults. A 60 kg senior needs roughly 60 to 72 g. See our full guide on how much protein senior citizens need. Here are the main vegetarian sources:

Smart vegetarian protein habits

  • Combine cereals with pulses: dal-roti, rajma-rice, khichdi and idli-sambar across the day.
  • Include milk, curd, paneer or tofu daily if you eat dairy or soya.
  • Add a protein snack: roasted chana, sprouts chaat, peanuts or a glass of buttermilk or milk.
  • Spread protein through breakfast, lunch, snack and dinner, not just at one meal.

Part 2: Why vitamin B12 matters

Vitamin B12 helps form red blood cells, protects nerves and supports the brain. The recommended amount for adults is just 2.4 micrograms a day, but getting it is harder than it sounds.

  • Only animal foods have B12 naturally. That means milk, curd, paneer, eggs, fish and meat. Plant foods have none unless they are fortified.
  • Older adults absorb it less well. Many have lower stomach acid, which makes it harder to release B12 from food. The NIH suggests people over 50 get most of their B12 from fortified foods or supplements.
  • Some medicines reduce absorption: long-term metformin for diabetes and acid-reducing medicines such as omeprazole.
  • It can creep up silently. The body stores a large amount, so symptoms may take years to appear.

Studies in India suggest B12 deficiency is common, especially in vegetarians. One small study of people with diabetes on metformin found that vegetarians were much more likely to have low or borderline B12 levels than non-vegetarians.

Possible signs of low B12

  • Persistent tiredness or weakness.
  • Numbness or tingling in the hands and feet.
  • Memory problems, low mood or confusion.
  • Pale skin, loss of appetite or unplanned weight loss.

These signs have many causes, including normal ageing and other illnesses. That is why you should see a doctor and get a blood test, not guess.

What to do about B12

  1. TestAsk your doctor for a vitamin B12 blood test, especially if you are vegetarian, over 60, or on metformin or acidity medicines.
  2. EatInclude milk, curd or paneer daily if you can tolerate them. Check labels for B12-fortified foods.
  3. Supplement if advisedYour doctor will decide if you need a tablet, an injection or fortified foods, and the right dose.
  4. RecheckGo back for a follow-up test as your doctor advises.
Sarita's tip 🌿

Write your medicines list on one page and take it with you to the doctor. Mention that you are vegetarian and ask, "Should my B12 be checked?"

Foods that are not reliable B12 sources

Do not count on dal, vegetables, grains, sprouts or seaweed for B12. Fermented foods like idli, dosa and dhokla should not be treated as dependable sources either. If you eat eggs or fish, they do provide B12, but your doctor can still advise whether your levels are enough.

Other nutrients to ask about

  • Vitamin D and calcium for bones: ask about a vitamin D test, and include milk, curd, paneer, ragi and greens.
  • Iron from dal, rajma, leafy greens and sesame, with a vitamin C food such as lemon, amla or guava.
  • Omega-3 from flaxseed, walnuts and mustard oil.

For sons and daughters

  • Help your parent book a B12 and vitamin D blood test.
  • Keep an up-to-date list of their medicines, especially metformin and acidity tablets.
  • Add one protein food to every meal. See the Indian Senior Plate.
  • Pair good food with strength work: strength training after 60.

Talk to your doctor or dietitian if

  • You have kidney, liver, heart or digestive conditions.
  • You have numbness, memory problems or unplanned weight loss.
  • You take metformin, acid-reducing medicines or many other tablets.
  • You want to start any vitamin or protein supplement.

Frequently asked questions

Can vegetarian seniors get enough protein?

Yes. Dal, rajma, chana, sprouts, soya chunks, tofu, paneer, milk and curd can meet protein needs if you include a good source at every meal and snack. Combining cereals with pulses, like khichdi or dal-roti, helps.

Do dal and vegetables provide vitamin B12?

No. Vitamin B12 occurs naturally only in animal foods such as milk, curd, paneer, eggs, fish and meat. Plant foods have none unless they are fortified, so dal and vegetables cannot be relied on for B12.

Is milk and curd enough for vitamin B12?

They contain some B12, but the amount depends on how much you have. Many older adults also absorb B12 poorly from food, so a doctor may advise a blood test and fortified foods or a supplement.

Should every vegetarian senior take a B12 supplement?

Not automatically. Vegetarians over 60, people on metformin or acidity medicines, and anyone with symptoms should ask their doctor for a blood test and advice. Your doctor will decide if you need a tablet, an injection or fortified foods, and the right dose.

Can low B12 cause numbness or memory problems?

Low B12 can be linked to tiredness, weakness, numbness or tingling in the hands and feet, and memory or mood changes. These symptoms have many causes, so see a doctor rather than self-treating.

Why does B12 deficiency take so long to show up?

The body stores a large amount of B12, so symptoms can take years to appear. That is why a blood test is more reliable than waiting for symptoms.
