Walking

Walking for Seniors in Indian Summer and Monsoon: Safe Tips

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 2 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • Walk in the cooler hours of summer, and indoors on very hot or very rainy days.
  • Sip fluids regularly, wear light clothes and non-slip shoes, and choose flat, familiar, well-lit paths.
  • Start with just 5 to 10 minutes, and check with your doctor first if you have a health condition.

Walking is the simplest exercise for seniors: no gym, no equipment and no experience needed. In India, though, the weather changes the rules. Summer heat and humidity can lead to dehydration and exhaustion, and the monsoon brings slippery floors, puddles and broken paths. This guide shows how to keep walking safely through every season, with a free 10-minute starter routine for the days you cannot go out.

Why walking is worth it

Walking is gentle, helps you stay active and builds the habit of daily movement. The WHO suggests older adults work toward 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, as health allows. Do not begin there. Your handbook-style starting point is just 5 to 10 minutes of outdoor walking as tolerated, or 3 to 5 minutes indoors, and then building up slowly.

Talk to your doctor first if

  • You have heart, lung or kidney conditions, or unstable blood pressure.
  • You feel dizzy, faint or breathless when you walk.
  • You have fallen recently or feel unsteady.
  • You take several medicines. Some can make you more sensitive to heat or cause dizziness.

Summer walking tips

  • Time it: walk early in the morning or after sunset. Avoid the hottest hours, roughly 10 or 11 am to 4 pm.
  • Dress for heat: light, loose, light-coloured cotton, a cap or umbrella, and closed, supportive shoes.
  • Drink early and often: thirst is not always a reliable signal as we age. Unless your doctor has restricted fluids, aim for roughly 1.5 to 2 litres of fluids a day, including water, buttermilk and nimbu pani without much sugar.
  • Ask about medicines: a doctor or pharmacist can tell you which ones increase heat sensitivity.
  • Go slowly: take rest breaks in the shade and shorten the walk on hot, humid days.
  • Walk with company and carry a phone, water and your emergency contact.

Know the heat warning signs

ConditionWarning signsWhat to do
Heat exhaustionHeavy tiredness, headache, muscle cramps, dizziness or lightheadedness, nausea, fast heartbeat, heavy sweating.Stop at once. Move to shade or indoors, sit or lie down, loosen clothes, cool the body and sip fluids. Seek medical help if it does not improve.
Heat stroke (emergency)Confusion, fainting, very hot skin, a sudden stop in sweating, severe headache, fast pulse.Call 112 or your local ambulance number now. Move to a cool place and cool the body while waiting.

Monsoon walking tips

  • Watch for slippery surfaces: wet marble, tiles, moss on steps, mud and wet leaves.
  • Wear shoes with good grip and keep them dry. Avoid loose slippers.
  • Skip waterlogged roads and open drains. Avoid wading through stagnant or flood water, which can carry infections. If your feet get wet, wash them with soap and clean water and dry them well.
  • Keep your hands free: use a raincoat, or a stable umbrella if you need it, not both hands full.
  • Mind the light: days get darker early, so walk while it is bright, or indoors.
  • Protect against mosquitoes around dusk.

Choose safe paths

  • Pick a flat, familiar and well-lit route.
  • Look out for broken pavements, kerbs, speed breakers and loose paving.
  • Do not rush, especially when turning or stepping off a kerb.
  • Use any walking aid your doctor has prescribed.
  • Do not walk while looking at your phone.

Walk indoors on bad weather days

On very hot or rainy days, walk along a clear, level corridor or hall with relaxed arms and comfortable breathing. Start with 3 to 5 minutes and build slowly. Keep loose rugs and wet floors out of your path. You can also try seated cardio arm movements: move your arms forward and back in a comfortable alternating rhythm for 30 to 60 seconds, rest, and repeat 2 to 5 times.

A 10-minute starter routine

If a full walk feels like too much, do this short routine and count it as a win.

  1. 2 minutesSeated marching and shoulder rolls.
  2. 3 minutesSit-to-stand, one set, using a sturdy chair.
  3. 2 minutesSupported heel raises, holding a fixed counter.
  4. 2 minutesComfortable breathing, in gently and out slowly.

For sons and daughters

  • Check the weather and your parent's footwear before they go out.
  • Walk with them when you can, or arrange a walking partner.
  • Make sure they carry a phone, water and an emergency contact.
  • Read our guide to helping elderly parents exercise safely and our fall-proofing checklist. For seated exercises, see chair exercises for seniors.

Stop right away if

  • Chest pain, fainting or severe dizziness.
  • Unusual shortness of breath or sudden new weakness.
  • Confusion, a sudden stop in sweating or signs of heat stroke.
  • Sharp pain that gets worse.

Stop and seek medical help straight away if any of these happen.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time for seniors to walk in summer?

Early morning or after sunset, when it is cooler. Avoid the hottest hours, roughly 10 or 11 in the morning to 4 in the afternoon, and check the temperature and humidity before you go out.

How long should a senior walk each day?

Start with 5 to 10 minutes as it feels comfortable, and build up gradually. Short walks split across the day count. The WHO's 150 to 300 minutes a week is a long-term goal, not where you must begin.

Is it safe to walk during the monsoon?

It can be, if you choose a dry, well-lit and familiar route, wear shoes with good grip and avoid wet marble, moss, mud and flooded or waterlogged roads. On heavy rain days, walk indoors instead.

How much water should seniors drink when walking?

Sip regularly through the day and before, during and after your walk, because thirst is not always a reliable signal in older age. Unless your doctor has restricted fluids, aim for roughly 1.5 to 2 litres of fluids a day, including buttermilk and nimbu pani without much sugar.

What should seniors wear for a walk?

Light, loose, light-coloured cotton clothes, a cap or umbrella in the sun, and closed, supportive, non-slip shoes. In the monsoon, wear a raincoat or carry a hands-free umbrella and keep your shoes dry.

Is indoor walking enough?

Yes. A clear, level corridor or hall is a good option on very hot or rainy days. Start with 3 to 5 minutes, build gradually, and keep rugs and wet floors out of your path.
