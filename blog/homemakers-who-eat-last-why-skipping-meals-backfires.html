Homemakers Who Eat Last: Why Skipping Your Own Meals Backfires and How to Fix It

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 22 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • Eating last, skipping breakfast and living on leftovers often leads to late-night hunger, tiredness and weight gain, even when you eat little.
  • The fix is not eating less. It is eating with your family, plating yourself first and having protein at every meal.
  • If weight or energy is off, ask your doctor about thyroid, anaemia and other causes. Get the free 3-day plan below.

You cook, serve, refill, clear and clean. By the time the family has finished, you eat whatever is left, standing in the kitchen, often at 3 pm or 10 pm. Then you wonder why you feel tired, why you are hungry at midnight and why the weight keeps creeping up even though you "hardly eat anything". If this sounds like you, you are not alone, and it is not a lack of willpower. It is a pattern, and it can be changed.

Does this sound like you?

  • You serve everyone first and eat what is left.
  • You skip breakfast, then have tea and biscuits until lunch.
  • You taste dishes while cooking all morning.
  • You finish your children's plates so food is not wasted.
  • You eat dinner at 10 pm or later, after the kitchen is clean.
  • You eat standing up, in a hurry, or while doing something else.

What the data says

  • India's National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) found that 57% of women aged 15 to 49 are anaemic, compared with 26% of men.
  • Indian nutrition research describes how women often eat last and least at home, and the leftovers they eat may be short of iron and protein.
  • Anaemia can occur together with overweight in the same woman. Tiredness, low appetite and thyroid changes linked to it can push weight either way.

In other words, being on your feet all day on little food is not the same as being healthy.

Why eating last backfires

  • Long gaps lead to big, late meals. Skipping meals can trigger intense hunger, and many people overeat later, often in the evening. In one small, carefully controlled study, eating later in the day increased hunger and shifted appetite hormones.
  • Leftovers are often low in protein and iron. You fill up on roti and rice, but miss the dal, curd and eggs that keep you full.
  • Invisible calories add up. Tasting, tea with biscuits and finishing the children's food rarely feel like a meal, but they count.
  • Low protein makes you tired and hungry. You crave sugar and carbs by the evening.
  • You move less when you are exhausted, and poor sleep and stress make hunger worse.

The evidence on meal timing is still developing and the studies are small, so I do not claim there is one perfect schedule. The practical lesson is simple: regular, real meals work better for most women than long gaps and late binges.

When it is not only food

If you are gaining weight on very little food, ask your doctor about:

  • Thyroid problems (tiredness, hair fall, feeling cold, weight gain).
  • Anaemia, low vitamin B12 or vitamin D (tiredness, breathlessness, pale skin).
  • PCOS or irregular periods.
  • Perimenopause, usually after 40.
  • Medicines that cause weight gain, poor sleep or high stress.

A doctor can tell you which blood tests are right. If you have ever had an eating disorder, please talk to a doctor before changing how you eat.

The fix: six simple rules

  1. Eat with the familySame meal, same time, sitting down. Even 15 minutes counts.
  2. Plate yourself firstPut your own plate together before you serve others, then sit.
  3. Protein at every mealDal, curd, paneer, egg, sprouts or soya on your plate each time.
  4. Never skip breakfastA quick option like besan chilla with curd, or poha with peanuts and an egg.
  5. Close the kitchen on timeAim to finish dinner 2 to 3 hours before bed.
  6. Plan two snacksRoasted chana, fruit or curd, so you are not grazing all day.

Instead of, try this

  • Eating whatever is left over→Plating your own meal before you serve
  • Skipping breakfast, then tea and biscuits→A 10-minute breakfast with protein
  • Tasting while cooking, all morning→A small bowl tasted once, then a real meal
  • Finishing the children's plates→Smaller portions for them, a proper plate for you
  • Dinner at 10.30 pm after cleaning→Eat first, clean after, with dinner 2 to 3 hours before bed
  • Eating standing at the counter→Sitting down for 15 minutes, even if you are last

Leftovers: the smart way

  • Cook a little extra dal or sabzi for yourself, so you have a proper portion, not scraps.
  • Add protein and a vegetable to leftover rice or roti: an egg, curd, paneer or a salad.
  • Keep portions for the children small, so you are not finishing plates.
  • Store food safely. Cool rice quickly, keep it in the fridge for no more than a day and reheat it only once.
Sarita's tip 🌿

Serve your plate first, and then everyone else's. It takes one minute, and it changes the whole day. You are not being selfish. You are the one who keeps the house running.

Getting your family on board

  • Ask for one thing at a time: "Let's all sit for dinner tonight."
  • Tell the family why: more energy means a happier house.
  • Give children small jobs, like putting out plates and water.
  • In a joint family, start with one meal a day and be polite and consistent.

Frequently asked questions

Is it okay to eat after my family?

Yes, if it is a proper meal with protein, not whatever is left over. The easiest fix is to sit down and eat together. If timing makes that impossible, put your own plate together before you serve everyone else.

Why do I gain weight even though I eat so little?

Several things can be at work: long gaps that lead to big late-night meals, unnoticed extra bites while cooking, tea and biscuits, low protein, poor sleep and stress. Thyroid problems, PCOS, anaemia, perimenopause and some medicines can also play a part, so ask your doctor about tests.

Is skipping breakfast bad?

Not always, but for many homemakers it leads to intense hunger and overeating later in the day. A simple breakfast with protein, like besan chilla with curd or an egg with poha, helps steady appetite and energy.

Are leftovers bad for me?

Leftovers are fine when stored safely and eaten as part of a balanced plate. The problem is when they become your whole meal, usually low in protein, eaten standing up. Plate them with a protein and a vegetable, and keep cooked rice for no more than a day in the fridge.

How can I change this in a joint family?

Start with small, polite steps. Ask to eat the first round together, serve your plate at the same time as others, and cook a little extra dal or sabzi for yourself. Most families adjust once they see you have more energy.

What if I am too tired in the evening and just want to snack?

Plan two small snacks with protein, like roasted chana or curd, so you are not grazing. A proper lunch and a snack at 4 pm make a late-night binge much less likely.
