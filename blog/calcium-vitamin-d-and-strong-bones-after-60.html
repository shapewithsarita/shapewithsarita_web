Bone health

Calcium, Vitamin D and Strong Bones After 60

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 11 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • Bones need calcium, vitamin D, protein and regular weight-bearing exercise.
  • Indian guidance for adults is about 1,000 mg of calcium a day, ideally from food.
  • Ask your doctor about a vitamin D test and bone health. Do not start supplements on your own.

Bones quietly lose strength as we age, and a fall that would once have been a bruise can become a fracture. The good news is that food, safe sunlight and the right kind of movement all help protect your bones. In India, low calcium intake and low vitamin D are both common, so it is worth paying attention. This guide shows how seniors and their families can look after bones, with Indian food sources and safe habits.

Why bones need attention after 60

  • Bone density gradually declines with age, especially in women after menopause.
  • Weak bones make falls more dangerous, with a higher risk of hip, wrist and spine fractures.
  • Osteoporosis often has no symptoms until a bone breaks.
  • Many seniors have low calcium intake or low vitamin D, and may not know it.

Calcium: how much and where from

Indian guidance suggests about 1,000 mg of calcium a day for adults. Aim to get most of it from food, spread through the day.

FoodHow to include it
Milk, curd, buttermilk, paneerPlain curd and buttermilk are often easier to digest than milk. Take 1 to 2 servings of milk or curd daily if you tolerate them.
Ragi (finger millet)Ragi roti, dosa, porridge or idli. A traditional, calcium-rich grain.
Sesame (til)A spoon of til chutney or powder with meals. A small amount goes a long way.
Green leafy vegetablesPalak, methi, amaranth (chaulai) and drumstick leaves. Cook lightly and eat often.
Small fish with bonesFor those who eat fish. Eat the soft bones.
Nuts, seeds and pulsesAlmonds, flax and pumpkin seeds, rajma, chana and soya add smaller amounts.
Fortified foodsCheck labels for added calcium and vitamin D.

Lactose intolerance is common in India. If milk upsets you, try curd, buttermilk, paneer, ragi, sesame, greens and small fish with bones, or ask about lactose-free milk.

Vitamin D: the partner that helps absorb calcium

Vitamin D helps your body absorb calcium and supports muscle and bone. India has plenty of sunshine, yet studies show vitamin D deficiency is common, including among older adults, in some studies more than half. Reasons include less time in the sun, covered skin, sunscreen, pollution, darker skin tones, and the skin making less vitamin D with age.

  • Sunlight on skin: the main natural source.
  • Food: eggs and fatty fish such as sardine and mackerel have some. Most vegetarian foods have very little, so fortified foods help.
  • Supplements: only as your doctor advises, after a blood test.

Safe sunlight tips

  • Short, regular time outdoors is better than long sessions. Many Indian experts suggest roughly 15 to 30 minutes, several times a week, with arms and legs uncovered.
  • Mid-morning sun is more useful for vitamin D than very early morning or evening, but avoid the hottest hours and never let your skin burn.
  • The right amount depends on skin tone, season, location and health, so ask your doctor.
  • Keep heat safety in mind. Carry water and rest in the shade. See our safe walking guide.

More than calcium: protect your bones every day

  • Protein: enough protein supports muscle and bone. See how much protein seniors need.
  • Movement: weight-bearing and strength exercises support bones. See strength training after 60.
  • Fall prevention: good balance and a safe home protect weak bones. See balance exercises and our fall-proofing checklist.
  • Lifestyle: do not smoke or chew tobacco, and avoid or limit alcohol.

If you have osteoporosis: move with care

Movement is still helpful, but it needs precautions. Avoid forceful bending or twisting of the spine, and skip movements that round your back under load. Seated exercises like cat-cow should be done in a very small range, or skipped, if you have osteoporosis or a spinal fracture. Ask your doctor or physiotherapist which exercises are safe.

A bone-friendly Indian day

  1. BreakfastRagi porridge, dosa or idli, with milk or curd.
  2. LunchDal, a green leafy sabzi such as palak or methi, roti and curd.
  3. EveningA glass of buttermilk or milk, with a few almonds.
  4. DinnerPaneer or tofu sabzi, or small fish with bones, with vegetables and roti. A spoon of til chutney on the side.
Sarita's tip 🌿

Spread calcium across the day: a little at breakfast, lunch, evening and dinner. A glass of milk, a katori of curd and a spoon of til chutney can add up without any tablets.

Tests and questions for your doctor

  • Should I get a vitamin D blood test?
  • Do I need a bone density scan?
  • Do any of my medicines, such as long-term steroids or acid-reducing tablets, affect my bones?
  • Do I need calcium or vitamin D supplements, and in what amount? Too much calcium is not better.
  • Which exercises are safe for me?

For sons and daughters

Help your parent book a vitamin D test and a bone check, keep the calcium-rich foods on the table, make sure the home is safe and walk or exercise together. Read our guide on helping elderly parents exercise safely.

See a doctor soon if

  • You have sudden severe back pain, or you are losing height.
  • You fall and then have pain in the hip, wrist or back.
  • You have bone pain, muscle weakness or frequent falls.
  • You are told you have osteoporosis and are unsure how to exercise.

Frequently asked questions

How much calcium do seniors need in a day?

Indian guidance for adults is about 1,000 mg of calcium a day, ideally from food such as milk, curd, paneer, ragi, sesame, green leafy vegetables and small fish with bones. Your doctor may advise a different amount for your needs.

Do I need a calcium supplement?

Not automatically. Food comes first. Supplements are only for people whose diet and medical needs call for them, and more is not better. Ask your doctor, because too much calcium can cause problems.

How can I get vitamin D in India?

From safe sunlight on the skin, eggs, fatty fish and fortified foods. Many vegetarians find it hard to get enough from food, and low vitamin D is common in India, so ask your doctor about a blood test and whether a supplement is needed.

Is sunlight alone enough for vitamin D?

Often not for older adults. Skin makes less vitamin D with age, and the amount depends on skin tone, season, clothing, sunscreen, pollution and time outdoors. Do not burn your skin, and ask your doctor what is right for you.

Can I build strong bones if I do not drink milk?

Yes. Curd and buttermilk, paneer, ragi, sesame, green leafy vegetables, nuts, pulses and small fish with bones all provide calcium. If you avoid dairy, a doctor or dietitian can help you plan enough calcium.

Can exercise improve bone health after 60?

Weight-bearing and strength exercises help support bone health, along with balance practice to reduce falls. If you have osteoporosis, follow movement precautions and avoid forceful bending or twisting of the spine.
