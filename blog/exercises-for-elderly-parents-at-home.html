Exercises for Elderly Parents at Home: How Sons and Daughters Can Help Safely

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
8 min readUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The short answer

  • Start with a 2-minute readiness check and your parent's doctor if the answers raise any worry.
  • Make the home safer, then begin with 5 to 10 minutes of gentle seated exercises.
  • Stay with them at first, praise effort and never push through pain.

You notice it when you visit: Papa gets up from the sofa slowly, Mummy avoids the stairs, and both say, "I am fine, beta." You want to help your parents stay strong and independent, but you do not want to nag or cause an injury. This guide shows sons and daughters how to help elderly parents exercise at home, one safe step at a time.

Start with a conversation, not a lecture

  • Ask what they want to keep doing: climbing stairs, visiting the temple, shopping, playing with grandchildren.
  • Talk about independence and comfort, not age or weakness.
  • Use words like "morning movement" if the word "exercise" puts them off.
  • Offer to do it together. Parents join more easily when you do.

Step 1: Do the 2-minute readiness check

Go through these questions with your parent. Tick Yes or No. This is a simple self-check, not a medical test.

How to read the answers

  • Yes to 3, 4, 5 or 6, or any doubt: speak to the doctor before starting.
  • Yes to 2, 7 or 8: start with seated and supported exercises and ask for personalised guidance.
  • No to most and Yes to question 1: your parent can usually begin gentle exercises at an easy level.

Step 2: Check with the doctor when needed

Tell the doctor which exercises you plan to try. Ask about medicines, blood pressure, diabetes and any movements to avoid. If your parent has osteoporosis, avoid forceful bending or twisting of the spine.

Step 3: Make the home safer

  • Loose rugs, trailing cords and clutter on the floor.
  • Chairs with wheels or light plastic chairs that slide.
  • Slippery bathroom floors, with no mat or grab bar.
  • Dark paths to the toilet at night.
  • Walking in socks or bare feet on smooth floors.

Remove or fix each one. Use a sturdy chair without wheels, closed non-slip footwear and a fixed counter for support.

Step 4: Begin with four gentle exercises

Start with 5 to 10 minutes. See our full chair exercises for senior citizens guide for more.

How to help during the session

  • Sit or stand beside your parent for the first few sessions.
  • Count the repetitions aloud and keep the mood light.
  • Praise effort and consistency, not how much they do.
  • Let them rest whenever they want to.
  • Watch their breathing, colour and balance.
Sarita's tip 🌿

Put on their favourite old songs or bhajans while they exercise. Music makes it feel like a pleasant routine, not a task.

Make it a family habit

  • Link it to something they already do, like after morning pooja or before the first cup of chai.
  • Invite the grandchildren to join for a few minutes.
  • Set small goals, such as 3 days this week.
  • Keep a simple weekly log and celebrate progress as a family.

If you live in another city or abroad

  • Fix a time for a short video call and exercise together on screen.
  • Arrange a local physiotherapist or helper to check their technique at the start.
  • Send a gentle reminder on WhatsApp instead of calling every day.
  • Send them a guide they can follow at their own pace, such as the Stronger After 60 handbook.

What not to do

  • Do not push your parent through pain or tiredness.
  • Do not compare them with other people.
  • Do not try balance tests without support nearby.
  • Do not skip the doctor when the readiness check says to.
  • Do not turn it into a daily argument.

When to stop and get help

  • Chest pain, fainting or severe dizziness.
  • Unusual shortness of breath or sudden new weakness.
  • Sharp pain that gets worse.

If any of these happen, stop the exercise and seek medical help straight away.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convince my parent to exercise?

Start with a conversation about what they want to keep doing, such as climbing stairs, visiting the temple or playing with grandchildren. Begin with a few minutes, do it together, praise effort and avoid pushing or nagging.

Should my parent exercise alone?

For the first few sessions, it is safer to have someone nearby, especially for standing exercises. Once your parent is confident, a doctor has approved and the space is safe, many seated routines can be done alone.

What if my parent has arthritis or osteoporosis?

Choose gentle, pain-free movements and ask the doctor or physiotherapist which ones to avoid. With osteoporosis, avoid forceful bending and twisting of the spine.

How long should each session be?

Start with 5 to 10 minutes and build up to 10 to 15 minutes. Short, regular sessions are better than long ones that tire your parent.

Is it safe if my parent has diabetes or high blood pressure?

Many people with these conditions can benefit from gentle movement, but it depends on the person and their medicines. Please get the doctor's advice first and watch for dizziness or unusual tiredness.

I live in another city or country. How can I help?

Set a fixed time for a video call and exercise together on screen. Arrange a local physiotherapist or helper for the first session, and send reminders with love, not pressure. You can also gift the Stronger After 60 handbook.
