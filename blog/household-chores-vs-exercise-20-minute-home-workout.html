Why Household Chores Don't Count as Exercise, and a 20-Minute Home Workout That Does

Sarita NawarCertified Nutrition & Fitness Coach
9 min readUpdated 22 Nov 2026

The short answer

  • Chores are real movement and worth counting, but most are light, uneven in effort and do not build strength.
  • Health guidance suggests 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week plus strength work on at least two days.
  • A 20-minute home workout, 3 days a week, fills the gap. No gym, no separate meals. Get the free 7-day plan below.

"I am on my feet from morning till night. Isn't that my exercise?" I hear this from homemakers almost every week, and I understand it. Cooking, cleaning, laundry, school runs and looking after everyone is hard, tiring work. But being busy and being fit are two different things. This guide explains, honestly and without judgement, what chores do for you, what they miss, and the simple 20-minute home workout that fills the gap.

What the numbers say about Indian women and exercise

  • In India's 2024 Time Use Survey, only 4.9% of women exercised on a given day, compared with 14.5% of men.
  • Among married people, 4.5% of women exercised, compared with 17.3% of men.
  • Women carry a larger share of unpaid household and care work, which leaves less time and energy.
  • Among people who did exercise, most did so for 30 minutes or less, and the peak time was around 6.30 am.

So this is not about laziness. It is about time. That is exactly why a short, well-planned routine matters.

Do chores count as exercise? The honest answer

Yes, chores count as physical activity. Researchers measure effort in METs, where sitting is 1. Anything from 3 to 6 METs is moderate intensity. Mopping or vacuuming at a brisk pace can reach that range, similar to a walk. But cooking, ironing and folding are light, and the effort varies a lot from person to person.

One large survey found that people who counted housework as exercise were not leaner than others, probably because they overestimate how hard or how long they work, or because they eat to match the tiredness. The researchers concluded that housework should not be seen as the main way of exercising. Your chores are a valuable bonus. They are just not the whole answer.

What your body actually needs

  • 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking.
  • Strength work on at least 2 days a week, for all the major muscles.
  • Short bursts count. Several 10-minute sessions work as well as one long one for many people.

Strength matters for homemakers in particular: it protects your back and knees when you lift children, buckets and groceries, and it keeps muscle and bone strong as you move toward your 40s and 50s.

The 20-minute home workout

You need a sturdy chair, a kitchen counter, a mat or folded dari, two water bottles and closed shoes. Wear anything comfortable.

  1. Minutes 0 to 3Warm-up: march on the spot, arm circles, hip circles, a few easy squats.
  2. Minutes 3 to 15Circuit: do the 4 moves below, one after another, for 3 rounds. Rest 30 to 45 seconds between rounds.
  3. Minutes 15 to 18Core and cardio: 2 counter planks of 20 to 30 seconds, then 90 seconds of fast marching.
  4. Minutes 18 to 20Cool-down: stretch calves, thighs and chest, and take 5 slow breaths.

Fit it into a homemaker's day

  • Use the pressure cooker. Three or four whistles give you about 10 to 12 minutes. Do half the workout while the dal cooks.
  • Pick your window. Early morning before the house wakes, after school drop or before the evening rush.
  • Split it. Two 10-minute blocks count too.
  • Put it on the calendar like a family appointment, with 3 fixed days.
  • Involve the family. Kids can do the bridges next to you.

Make your chores work a bit harder

  • Sweep and mop at a brisk, steady pace, and switch sides often.
  • Bend from the hips with a straight back when you pick things up.
  • Carry shopping evenly in both hands.
  • Do calf raises while you stand at the stove.

These small habits help, but they do not replace the workout.

Start safely

Please check with your doctor first if any of these apply:

  • You are pregnant, or recently gave birth. Wait for approval, usually 6 weeks after a vaginal birth and longer after a C-section.
  • You have a heart condition, high blood pressure or dizziness.
  • You have joint, back or pelvic floor problems, such as leaking or a feeling of heaviness.
  • You have had recent surgery or an injury.

Stop and get medical help for chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness or sharp, worsening pain.

Frequently asked questions

Do household chores count as exercise?

They count as physical activity, and some, like mopping and vacuuming, reach moderate intensity. But chores rarely include strength work, vary a lot in effort and repeat the same movements. They are a good bonus, not a replacement for a workout.

Is 20 minutes enough?

For many beginners, yes. Three or four 20-minute sessions a week, plus walking and your daily chores, is a solid start. Health guidance suggests building toward 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, with strength work on at least two days.

How many days a week should I work out?

Start with 3 days and add a 4th when it feels easy. Keep at least one rest day, and do not train the same muscles hard on consecutive days.

Do I need equipment?

No. You need a sturdy chair, a kitchen counter, a mat or folded dari, and two water bottles. Wear closed, non-slip shoes.

What if I miss a day or a week?

Just restart with your next planned session. Do not try to make up for it by doing double. Consistency over months matters more than a perfect week.

Can I start after pregnancy, or with a health condition?

Not without checking first. After delivery, wait for your doctor's approval, usually 6 weeks after a vaginal birth and longer after a C-section. Also check with your doctor if you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, joint problems or dizziness.
